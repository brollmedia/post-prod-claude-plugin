# Story
Read this for a full-frame piece over 15 s, or any time a graphic has to carry an idea and not only a fact.
Contents: The one thing | Three storylines | The first frame | Beats | Shots | Storyboard | Copy

## The one thing
Finish the sentence "the viewer leaves knowing ___". Then find the one real fact that proves it, and carry that fact from the first frame to the payoff. A number earned is worth more than a number shown: show what it took before you show the total. If the fact isn't there, ask for it. Never invent one. With only two numbers, a line between them is an illustration: label its ends and nothing in between.

## Three storylines
Write three routes through the material in a line each: the obvious one and two that surprise. Pick the strongest yourself and name the others in your reply in a sentence. Don't stop to ask: a first result beats a question.

## The first frame
Frame 0 is the thumbnail on social and the first impression everywhere else, so it is never empty, black or a logo. It already carries the subject: the first place on the route, the starting number, the first line of the quote.

## Beats
Start at 10% hook, 60% middle, 20% payoff, 10% landing, then fit it to the words. Each beat changes one thing and the next beat answers it. A piece that only appears and holds has no beats.

## Shots
For each shot write a line: frames, hero move, how long the words have to be read, and how it hands off to the next. Speech runs at about 2.2 words a second, so count the script against the time before animating.

## Storyboard
On anything over 15 s, render one still per shot first and look at them as a set: `still --t`. With a review link, send the stills as a version and take notes on the frames: only the noted frames are redone. It costs a minute and saves the render.

## Copy
Say the specific thing. "3,200 km" beats "an epic journey". Never: unlock, seamless, elevate, game-changing, revolutionise. If a line would fit any video, cut it.
